# R1 — analytical boundary, not a second GRPO implementation

Source: [DeepSeek-R1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948). Catalogue records v2;
the original R1-Zero/R1 distinction is the scope of this note. The implementation
is explicitly the earlier [Math-v1 subset](math.md), not a reproduction of R1 v2.

R1-Zero starts RL without the preliminary SFT used in this lab. R1's staged recipe
includes cold-start data, reasoning-oriented RL, rejection-based data selection
and subsequent training/alignment. Distilled checkpoints involve outputs from a
teacher and student training; generated exact arithmetic labels are not teacher
outputs. Reusing GRPO alone implements none of those paper-specific stages.

Equation map and shapes: `group_advantages` [Q,G] and `grpo_loss` [Q,G,L] in
[grpo.py](../deepseek_minimal/grpo.py) correspond to the reused grouped relative
policy optimizer; [math.md](math.md) fixes the precise version and conventions.
`python -m examples.grpo` and [results](../results/grpo.json) are evidence only for
that optimizer/task subset. The later [model extension](models.md) adds the V3
backbone, dense students and an explicit training-stage analogue. Neither experiment
claims emergent reasoning, chain-of-thought behavior, released teacher knowledge,
full recipe reproduction or a Lean result.

Inferred limitation/hypothesis for a future R1 pipeline analogue: verified
rejection selection might improve student label correctness but discard diverse
valid solutions. Costs include teacher generation and verification; biased
selection can narrow the training distribution. Compare student exact correctness
and solution diversity with equal training-token budgets; failure on either
prespecified criterion can reject the hypothesis. Rejection selection and
distillation are already in R1 and other work. This is a planned ablation, not
novel research or an implemented extension.
